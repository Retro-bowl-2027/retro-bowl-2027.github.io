Bloxors – Tip the block end over end into the hole

Somewhere across a platform hanging in space, a square hole waits. Your piece is a tall block, one tile wide and two tall, and the only way to move it is to tip it over. Damien Clarke's 2007 Flash puzzle ends each level when the block stands upright over the hole and drops through.

The basics of Bloxors

The arrow keys roll the block one step in any direction. Standing up, it covers one tile; lying down, it covers two. That difference decides everything, because the hole only accepts a standing block. Later levels add orange tiles that give way under an upright block, switches that open or close bridges, and pads that split the block into two cubes you move in turn. Roll off the edge and the level restarts.

Getting started

  • Before moving, trace a rough route from the block to the hole.
  • Count rolls so the block arrives standing, since lying flat over the hole does nothing.
  • Lie the block down to cross orange tiles safely.
  • Write down each stage code so you can return to it later.

Tips from experience

Work backward. Picture the block standing on the hole, then ask which tile it must have come from, and which before that. Learn the rhythm of a straight roll: the block alternates between standing and lying, covering three tiles every two moves. Use sideways rolls to shift position by one tile.

Who should play it

One of the best pure logic puzzles from the Flash years, suited to anyone who enjoys spatial thinking. There is no time pressure. Later levels get long, and one slip near the end means starting that level again.
