Grey Box Testing – Puzzle rooms that look unfinished on purpose

Plain grey walls, simple blocks and no decoration at all. Sam Hogan's Unity puzzle game looks like a level someone forgot to finish, and that is the joke. Grey boxes are how designers test ideas before adding art. Here the test environment is the game, and every room hides a puzzle built from its basic pieces.

The basics of Grey Box Testing

Walk through each room, look at what it contains and figure out how the pieces behave. Some elements move, some react to you and some combine in unexpected ways. Experimenting is the only way to learn the rules. Solve a room to move on to the next, where the same building blocks return in trickier arrangements.

Getting started

  • Explore the whole room before you touch anything.
  • Interact with each element once to learn what it does.
  • Note how pieces react to each other, not only to you.
  • Revisit an earlier idea when a later room seems impossible.

Tips from experience

Treat each room as a lab. Form a hypothesis about how an element works, test it and update your understanding. The grey look hides nothing; the solution is always built from what you can see. If you are stuck, try combining two elements you have only used separately. Clever solutions often look obvious once found.

Who should play it

Great for puzzle fans who enjoy experimentation and a dry sense of humor. The minimal look keeps focus sharp. Players who want rich visuals or story will find it bare by design.
