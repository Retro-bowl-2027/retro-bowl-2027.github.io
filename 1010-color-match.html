1010 Color Match – Place plates and pair up the colors

Potato Jam took the familiar block-placing board and added color to the rules. Pieces arrive as colored plates. Drag them onto the grid and set matching colors next to each other to clear them away. The board is large, but it fills faster than it looks, and every badly placed plate costs you room you will want later.

The basics of 1010 Color Match

You get a few plates at a time and drop them anywhere they fit. When matching colors meet, those plates are eliminated and space opens up. Your score grows with each clear. The game ends when nothing you hold fits on the board. Planning ahead and grouping colors keeps the grid open.

Controls

  • Mouse – drag / Drop

Getting started

  • Look at every plate in your tray before placing the first one.
  • Give each color its own zone of the board.
  • Leave gaps the size of your common pieces, not odd single cells.
  • Drop a plate only when you know where its match will land.

Tips from experience

Think in color zones. If reds live in one corner and blues in another, most new plates have an obvious home. Keep the middle open as long as you can, because edges are easier to fill neatly. Do not place a plate just because it fits; a poor drop can strand a color with no partner nearby. When the board tightens, pick the clear that frees the biggest area.

Who should play it

A calm puzzle for players who like 1010-style games but want an extra layer of planning. It works well with a mouse and needs no fast reflexes. Long sessions can feel samey, since the core loop rarely changes.
