Cubefield – Thread a tiny ship through a field of cubes

A small triangle of a ship, an endless plain and cubes scattered everywhere. Nils Asejevs made Cubefield in 2006, and it became a fixture of early web game sites. There is one rule: hit nothing. The ship only goes forward, the cubes only multiply, and your nerve decides how far you get.

The basics of Cubefield

Forward speed is out of your hands; only left and right belong to you. Cubes stand in the way, loosely scattered at first and later packed into walls with slim openings. The faster you go, the less warning each cube gives, and the field switches colors and layouts in stages. Touch one cube and the run is done. Distance traveled is your score.

Getting started

  • Ride the center line, where both sides remain reachable.
  • Choose your opening while it is still on the horizon.
  • Tap the keys briefly; holding them sends the ship sliding too far.
  • Expect a tighter layout every time the colors switch.

Tips from experience

Distance is your friend. Cubes that are already close leave no time for a clean move, so commit to openings while they are tiny dots. Slide across, then straighten out before the next one. The edges of the field tend to box you in. Late in a run, inputs should be brief and smooth. Loosen your grip; tension produces oversteer.

Who should play it

A classic for anyone who likes pure reflex games and early web history. It runs on nearly any machine. Its simplicity is the whole package, so players who want progression will drift away.
