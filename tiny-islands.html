Tiny Islands – Small islands, careful eyes, quiet puzzles

Each challenge in Tiny Islands fits on a patch of land you could cover with your hand. David King's 2019 Unity WebGL puzzler is quiet and unhurried, with no clock and no penalty for slow thinking. What it asks for is attention. Notice how the pieces on each island relate, and the right move usually follows.

The basics of Tiny Islands

An island holds a few objects and spaces. Look at how they connect, then work out the action or sequence that resolves the arrangement. The answer often hinges on a detail that is easy to skim past. Solve one island and the next appears. With no timer, you can turn each problem over for as long as you like.

Getting started

  • Look at the whole island before touching anything.
  • Note any pattern or repeated shape among the pieces.
  • Try one change at a time and watch the result.
  • Step back when stuck and look for a missed detail.

Tips from experience

Slow down. Rushing tends to hide the answer rather than reveal it. View each island from every angle the game allows. When an idea fails, ask what the failure told you before discarding it. The solution is often simpler than it first appears, and a short break can make it obvious.

Who should play it

Suits players who like calm, observation-based puzzles. It is gentle and unhurried. Players wanting fast action or long challenges may find it too quiet and short.
