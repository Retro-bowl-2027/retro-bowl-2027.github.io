Go Ball – Get the ball home through gravity and guesswork

Go Ball pits one ball against one level, and gravity is the referee. Whatever you set in motion, the ball rolls with the slope, picks up pace, glances off corners and tumbles through gaps. Your work happens before any of that: picture the whole run, then make it happen. Get the route right and the ball drops into its goal.

The basics of Go Ball

Each level is a small physics problem made of slopes, walls, gaps and other pieces. You change the level in some way to steer the ball's path, then let physics play out. A good plan sends the ball into the goal; a poor one leaves it stuck or lost. Momentum matters, so the speed a ball carries into each obstacle is as important as its direction.

Getting started

  • Before acting, follow the route you expect the ball to take with your eyes.
  • Mark mentally which slopes will speed it up and which will sap its pace.
  • Alter just one detail per try so the effect is obvious.
  • Note the exact spot where a failed ball comes to rest; it points to the fix.

Tips from experience

Guessing wastes attempts. Build a theory of the ball's route, then put it to the test. Arrival speed is easy to misjudge: too quick and it sails past, too slow and it stops short. Edges and corners deflect it more than you expect. When a plan nearly works, tune it gently instead of starting from nothing.

Who should play it

Good for players who like thoughtful, low-pressure physics puzzles. Levels are short. Its plain presentation means it relies entirely on the puzzles, which may not hold everyone.
