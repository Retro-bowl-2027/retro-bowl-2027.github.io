Space Garden – A small space game that rewards curiosity

Ryan Malm's Space Garden is the kind of tiny indie piece that explains almost nothing and trusts you to poke at it. Set among the stars, it is an HTML5 action game where things gradually take root and spread as you play. There is no long campaign. The pleasure comes from figuring out, round by round, which of your actions makes what happen.

The basics of Space Garden

Objects and events show up on screen and ask for a response. At first the rules are unclear, and that is deliberate: each attempt teaches you a little more about how the pieces connect and what feeds the growth. Picking it up takes seconds. Understanding it fully takes a few rounds of experiments and some careful watching.

Getting started

  • Start a round and watch how the screen responds to your input.
  • Try each action deliberately to see what it changes.
  • Play a second round using what you learned in the first.
  • Take note of anything that seems to grow or build up over time.

Tips from experience

Experiment rather than rush. Each round shows a little more of how the game works. When something surprises you, repeat it to understand why it happened. Keep sessions short and come back with fresh eyes, since patience reveals more than fast play ever will.

Who should play it

Suits curious players who enjoy small indie experiments. It is easy to try. Anyone looking for clear goals and long progression may find it too brief and too open.
