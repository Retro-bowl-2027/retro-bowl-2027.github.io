Kitten Cannon – Fire the kitten and let the field decide the rest

A cannon, a cartoon kitten and a field scattered with things that either help or hurt. Dan Fleming's 2005 Flash classic is one of the early launcher games, built on a single shot. Set the angle, set the power, fire, and watch the kitten bounce, tumble and sometimes get a huge boost on its way across the screen.

The basics of Kitten Cannon

Choose the cannon's angle and power, then launch. Once the kitten is airborne, the objects it lands on decide what happens next. Some send it flying further, others stop it cold. There is no steering after the shot. The distance it travels before coming to rest is your score, and because so much depends on where it lands, no two launches play out the same.

Getting started

  • Try a medium angle around forty-five degrees for your first shot.
  • Adjust power in small steps to find a good baseline distance.
  • Note which objects boost the kitten and which stop it.
  • Fire many times; luck plays a big part in each launch.

Tips from experience

Find a reliable launch and repeat it. A strong, mid-angle shot gives you the best chance of landing on helpful objects. Luck decides a lot after that, so treat it as a numbers game and take plenty of shots. Watch which zones tend to send the kitten farther. Do not overthink it; this is a classic for quick laughs.

Who should play it

A fun piece of Flash history for anyone who enjoys launcher games. Each shot takes seconds. The cartoon violence toward a kitten may bother some players, and the depth is very limited.
