Hextris – Spin the hexagon and match the falling colors

Blocks slide inward from six directions toward a hexagon in the middle of the screen. Logan Engstrom's 2014 puzzle, inspired by Tetris, turns stacking into rotation. Spin the hexagon so each incoming block lands on a side where its color can join others. Three or more together and they vanish. Let the stacks grow too tall and the game ends.

The basics of Hextris

Rotate the hexagon left or right as blocks slide in from the edges. When a block arrives, it stacks on whichever side faces it. Group three or more blocks of the same color in contact and they clear, sometimes triggering combos. Blocks arrive faster the longer you survive. If any side's stack reaches the outer boundary, the game is over.

Getting started

  • Watch the incoming blocks and rotate before they arrive.
  • Keep each side of the hexagon dominated by one or two colors.
  • Clear tall stacks first to stay away from the boundary.
  • Use small rotations; spinning too far sends blocks to the wrong side.

Tips from experience

Plan rotations ahead. Blocks approach from several directions at once, so think about where the next two will land before the first arrives. Spread colors so every side has matching options. Combos clear more space, so setting up a chain is worth a short wait. When a side gets dangerously tall, shrinking it comes before everything else.

Who should play it

Ideal for puzzle fans who like a fast twist on classic block games. The visuals are clean and calm. It gets intense quickly, which can overwhelm newcomers at first.
