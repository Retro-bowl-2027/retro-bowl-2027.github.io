World's Hardest Game – Red square, blue dots, endless retries

Stephen Critoph's December 2007 Flash game earned its bold name honestly. A tiny red square must thread its way through swarms of blue dots that sweep back and forth on fixed routes. Every yellow coin on the level has to be grabbed before the green exit counts. One brush with a dot and the level restarts. A counter tallies every failure.

The basics of World's Hardest Game

Each level is a compact arena with a green starting pad, a green goal and blue dots in between. Steer the red square out, collect every yellow coin, then reach the goal. Some levels include green pads in the middle that act as safe resting spots. Contact with a dot resets the level. Memorizing the dots' movement is the only reliable way across.

Getting started

  • Watch the dots cycle a few times before leaving the starting pad.
  • Move in short, deliberate bursts and pause in the gaps.
  • Plan a coin order that keeps you as far from the dots as possible.
  • Expect many failures, and treat each one as information.

Tips from experience

The dots never change their routes, so learn them. Wait for an opening instead of forcing one. Use short taps rather than long holds when you are close to the dots. Map your coin route before you set off. Let the death counter climb without worrying about it; everyone's does.

Who should play it

Ideal for players who enjoy precision challenges and stubborn tests of patience. Its reputation for difficulty is earned. Players who dislike repeated failure should probably steer clear.
